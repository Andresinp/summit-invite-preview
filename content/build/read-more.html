The problem

You are buying the same knowledge twice, and the invoice shows it.

Every day your agents burn tokens rereading the same code, the same systems, the same decisions — twenty years of your know-how, kept in fossil form. The deeper the adoption, the faster the spend climbs.

You pay again

The same understanding, bought twice. Once in company salaries. Again on AI per task.

You gain nothing

The year ends with the AI knowing exactly what it knew when it began. Nothing you spent on it compounds.

You lose it

Your know-how trains a model you do not own, and walks out when the engineer does.

Nothing you spend on it accumulates. That is the reread tax, and it is metered.

Why a better model does not fix it

A stronger model rebuilds your context faster. It still rebuilds it. Quality sets the price of the rebuild, not whether there is one. We sit above whichever model you buy — no bet on which lab wins.

What two years of AI budgets bought

Rising spend has returned less than promised almost everywhere. Not too early, not too slow — most of the money goes to code, and most of those tokens to rereading context the agent had yesterday.

What happens live

October 5, 15:10. Contextful is what stops it. One real task, run live, with the token count on screen.

  • The task One complex problem to solve. The same one for both sides.
  • The setup Our layer on a basic open-source model, against the frontier labs. Ordinary hardware. Live, and auditable from the floor.
  • The result Nothing gets reread, because the know-how is already kept, so the token count collapses to a fraction of what the same task costs you today.

The model did not get smarter. It stopped rediscovering what the organization already knew.

How it runs

On-premise, above any model you choose, and the models stay interchangeable. Your data, your tokens and your accumulated learning remain yours.

The tokens do not get cheaper. They stop being spent.
