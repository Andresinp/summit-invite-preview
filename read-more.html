THE CONTEXTFUL SUMMIT

The missing half of intelligence.

The problem

You are buying the same knowledge twice, and the invoice shows it.

Your organization spent years learning how its own systems work — in salaries, in outages, in decisions nobody wrote down. It owns that understanding. Then it bought AI, and began paying for it a second time, on a meter, every day.

Cost

Every task starts from nothing. Last week's work is done again, and billed again.

Learning

The year ends with the AI knowing exactly what it knew when the year began.

Control

What your organization knows is the part competitors cannot copy; for a country, it is sovereignty. Today it leaves in the traffic, and funds a model you do not own.

Two years of rising AI budgets have returned less than promised almost everywhere. The technology was not too early; the teams did not need longer. The money bought real work, and none of it stayed.

Nothing you spend on it accumulates. That is the reread tax, and it is metered.

The question is not whether the model is smart enough, but whether what it works out for you stays. Fix that and the same budget reaches further, and each system moves faster than the last. It sits above the models you already buy, so none of it is a bet on which lab wins.

Why a better model does not fix it

A stronger model rebuilds your context faster. It still rebuilds it. Model quality changes the price of the rebuild, not whether there is one.

Where the learning comes from

Every organization keeps an unintentional archive: twenty years of decisions, corrections and repairs, recorded down to the character. The explanations rotted — the commit says "fixed this," the engineer who understood the module left in 2019. The changes did not. Know-how in fossil form, and what the layer learns from.

What happens live

October 5. We run the experiment in front of you.

Every quarter something is announced that changes everything. This one you can check in the room: tell us which part of your estate costs you most to keep running, and that is what goes on the screen.

  • The task One complex problem to solve. The same one for both sides.
  • The setup Our layer on a deliberately weak open-source model, against the frontier labs. Ordinary hardware. Live, and auditable from the floor.
  • The result It finishes on many times fewer tokens, because nothing already learned is worked out twice.

Watch the path, not the finished code. The model did not get smarter; it stopped rediscovering what your organization already knew.

The measured figures are read out in the room, and nowhere before it. You hear it a day before the world, and get that day to take it apart.

How the run is set up

A basic open-source model: no tools, low reasoning, weak on purpose. What is tested is not its strength but what the layer above it keeps. Same task and hardware both sides, open to challenge from the floor.

How it runs

On-premise, above any model you choose, and the models stay interchangeable. Your data, your tokens and your accumulated learning remain yours.

The tokens do not get cheaper. They stop being spent.

The launch

Seven sessions over two days, and the people in them saying what they do not say publicly.

The live run is the centre of it, not the whole of it. Around it are seven sessions across two days in San Francisco: the stakes, sovereignty and control, what to do with legacy estates, the economics of tokens, then founders, then the investors who fund them.

No middle management. No press. That is what makes the sessions worth sitting in — the people on stage say the part they leave out in public, and they say it to fifty people who can act on it.

The room

The people who decide how AI enters an industry or a country: chief executives and boards, leadership of governmental governance authorities, top-tier funds, and the integrators running the world's largest AI programmes for other people.

The organizations in this room start in October. The ones that are not will spend the quarter working out what happened.
